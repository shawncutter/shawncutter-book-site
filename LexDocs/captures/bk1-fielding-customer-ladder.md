# The Fielding customer ladder

## Verbatim (Shawn, chat 2026-10-31)

> "I'll go through the big milestones of fielding getting first customer Cutter Oil running, the next customer was a FieldVisor customer, and I was really set up, so FieldVisor or SkateAdvisor could be the lead in, but the goal was to get them with everything. And then I got a saltwater disposal operation. Picked up a few Texas measurement companies and really just pieced together smaller deals. Until we landed Penn, Virginia."

> "once we landed in Penn, Virginia, we had a public company multi-billion-dollar enterprise value, and multiple regions of production and non-working non-operated working interest in they also marketed their own gas. It was a major rollout. And really tested us. The next big customer was Gulfport, and that really set the company. Also, multi-billion-dollar company, shale production all over. And that is what took us to an exit."

## The go-to-market, in one line of his

> "FieldVisor or [ScadaVisor] could be the lead in, but **the goal was to get them
> with everything.**"

That is land-and-expand, stated plainly, and it is the **commercial** half of the
"platform play" he described for the build. Two products as doors, one platform as
the sale. Worth noting it corroborates the platform framing from a completely
different direction — he said it about engineering first and about selling second,
on separate occasions, without being asked to reconcile them.

**The Texas measurement companies partly answer an open question.** `codes.FIELD`
had a gap asking what measurement's commercial use was — custody transfer,
allocation, or operational metering. Measurement companies *as customers* means
measurement was a market in its own right, which points at the service-company
side rather than at operators metering their own wells. Not conclusive, and the
gap stays open, but it is a real narrowing.

## Checks against outside sources (2026-10-31)

**Both company names are real, public, and verifiable.**

- **Penn Virginia Corporation** — independent oil and gas company, SEC filer
  (CIK 0000077159), 10-Ks for FY2012 and FY2014 on EDGAR. Its FY2012 10-K
  describes operations in **Texas, the Mid-Continent and Mississippi**, which
  supports his "multiple regions of production". Its filings also confirm it
  **sold its own gas** to interstate and midstream pipelines, priced off Henry
  Hub — his "they also marketed their own gas" checks out.
- **Gulfport Energy Corporation** — public, Utica Shale operator. 2014 production
  rose **255% over 2013**, and an (undated) company presentation shows
  **market cap $4.0B / enterprise value $4.6B**, supporting "multi-billion-dollar
  company". The timing fits exactly: Gulfport's breakout year was 2014 and the
  Quorum acquisition closed **March 2015**.

**Three of his descriptive details are NOT verified. Do not print them as fact:**

1. **Penn Virginia's "multi-billion-dollar enterprise value."** No clean dollar
   EV figure was found for 2012 or 2014. A 2012 note gives only valuation
   *ratios*. Plausible, unconfirmed — and a number a reader can look up.
2. **Penn Virginia's "non-operated working interest."** Not found described that
   way in its filings, which instead describe joint-venture participation in the
   Eagle Ford and Granite Wash. Adjacent, not the same thing.
3. **Gulfport's "shale production all over."** Gulfport was heavily
   **concentrated** in the Utica — Oil & Gas Journal reports the Utica at **97% of
   net 2Q production**. "All over" is loose; "almost entirely Utica" is the
   stronger and more impressive sentence anyway.

## The Penn Virginia relationship (Shawn, chat 2026-10-31)

> "Harry Harpold was the Penn Virginia contact. we built a relationship over a year before he decided to move forward. each time we connected, we had more custoemrs. that built confidence"

**This is the mechanism of the whole ladder, and it reframes the dull middle of
it.** The capture above lists "pieced together smaller deals" as a holding phase
between the small customers and the big one. It was not. Those deals were the
*argument*: over more than a year, every time Fielding came back to Harpold it
was a bigger company than the time before, and the growth itself did the
convincing.

So the saltwater disposal operation and the Texas measurement companies are not
filler on the way to Penn Virginia — they are **why Penn Virginia said yes**. For
the book that inverts the obvious structure: the unglamorous deals are the plot,
and the enterprise logo is the consequence.

It also pairs with `bk1-the-cold-call.md` (`ready`), whose Cutter Rule is *"Learn
the language of the room before you try to change what happens in it."* The
Harpold year is the same lesson at a longer timescale — not a cold call that
lands, but a year of returning with more proof each time.

**Timing, uncomputed on purpose:** "over a year before he decided to move
forward" dates the start of the relationship to roughly a year before the Penn
Virginia rollout, but the rollout itself has no date on file yet, so no year is
inferred here.
