I was venting. The CEO was making the wrong decisions, and I said so to the one person I knew would actually listen: Martin.

He didn't argue with me about the decisions. He told me things were going as planned. Just because it wasn't growing as fast as I thought it could didn't mean it was wrong.

That was enlightening. I had been measuring the company against the speed in my own head, and Martin was measuring it against the plan.

Then he said the part I didn't expect.

"You've done your part. You can leave if you want to."

By then Quorum had already acquired three companies. It had switched to subscription SaaS. It had working cloud solutions — mine and others. By any honest scoreboard, the thing I had come in to do was done.

I didn't leave. I doubled down. I decided to push as hard as I could, and if they fired me for doing the right thing, so be it.

It didn't feel like relief. It felt like loss.
