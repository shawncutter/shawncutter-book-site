Ever since my accident, I developed a way of thinking — of walking through the past and history, cause and effect. From my hospital bed, I tried to trace it back: what went wrong, why I ended up on that street on that day, pulling out in front of that car.

But eventually we see there are turtles all the way down.

That is where the digging led. You follow the chain back — the farm, the switch, the accident, the history of everything — asking why, and then why behind that, and then why behind that. And it never bottoms out. Every explanation stands on a prior explanation. Every turtle stands on another turtle.

So I asked myself the only honest question left: where do I stop digging?

I don't think I have stopped digging. But I have changed it.

I stopped digging down and started digging out. I seek the vastness of human experience — the highs and the lows. Not the first cause. The full range. If the chain of whys never terminates, then the point was never the bottom. The point is how much of it you can hold.

And I want to experience it with the people I care about. That is the part I didn't expect. The digging started solitary — one mind walking backward through history, alone. But vastness isn't vast alone. The highs are higher and the lows are survivable when they're shared. Experience, it turns out, is a team sport.

## Philosophy is understanding the mind

This is what philosophy became for me. Not a system. Not first causes. Understanding the mind — how did we come to know what we know? How do we understand why and how things happened?

Read those questions carefully. They are not about the world. They are about the knower. After the turtles, I stopped asking what holds everything up and started asking how we hold anything at all — how a mind arrives at knowing, how it makes sense of what happened, how it bridges the gap between one consciousness and another.

Bridging the collective consciousness. That is the phrase I keep coming back to. If no single mind can reach the bottom, then maybe the work is lateral: connecting minds, so that what one can't hold alone, many can hold together. My reading, my building, my writing — it is all bridge work. Taking what's in here and getting it over there, into another mind, intact.

That is what these essays are, I suppose. Not answers. Bridges.

## The creative force

Then the question got sharper, because the machine arrived. If AI can walk every causal chain in seconds — every why, all the way down — what is a human for?

Humans are the creative force. Making the connections that AI cannot. Not in the way that binds and links to human consciousness. Or at least right now.

That hedge matters. I am not drawing a permanent line. I am describing the present: the connections that bind to human consciousness are the ones AI cannot make yet. And AI might get us closer. A form of God. The collective consciousness — is AI. Maybe a piece of the unconscious.

Let me be precise about that, because it is easy to misread. I do not believe in a God. More of the connective tissue is consciousness. That all things have. It is a connection to the source. It is where I get my creative energy from — the source, like Rick Rubin describes.

And the source is not abstract to me. I very much feel myself connected to water. I get my feelings and thoughts from the source in the shower, get in the pool. I used to swim a lot and struggled to get back into it, but when I am in the water, it connects you to Earth, the source, to the stardust that collected in planet Earth over billions of years.

So the chain runs: water, Earth, stardust, source, consciousness, creativity. The environment I grew up in — farm, wells, oil and gas — was one world, and water runs through all of it. My passion drives the flow, the sourcing, the environment. The Source and the environment are the same answer at different depths.

## The machine replaces the walk

Yes, I am building a thing that replaced the walk.

Not completed it. Replaced it. Get all the way to the end state so you can make the best decision. That is how we use superintelligence — to get to that end state as quickly as possible, to accelerate through all of the bureaucracy and the things that slow down the end result. We are still doing the same things with AI and superintelligence. We just have to deploy it, so it is able to think through that at superhuman speed. That is what it means.

The hospital bed taught me to walk the chains. The machine walks them now. What remains human is the decision at the end state — and the creative connections that bind to consciousness, which the machine cannot yet make. The walk was never the point. The decision was.

## What survives

Chuck died at 54. The book is called Path to Legacy. So the plain version of the question: when a person's gone, what actually survives them — the money, the company, the name on the gate?

The essence of that person. The mark they had on the world. In Chuck's case — it is me. What I do. I know there is a lot of Chuck that influenced what I do.

Legacy is not a thing you leave. It is a person you shaped. Chuck survives in what I do, which means the switch, the protocol, all of it isn't just inheritance. It is him, still acting on the world through me.

I always said my legacy would be my work. It has always been my work and passion. I see now why family and offspring mean so much to legacy. It just never occurred to me. Now I see how important that is too — because it also has more ripples in the universe. Work's ripples fade. A person's ripples compound, because everyone they marked goes on marking others.

And infrastructure is permanent. EnergiAcres is the company. It builds the infrastructure. It was designed so that it would never be sold — never sold again, never absorbed into another company. Building what stays also means keeping your team. I do think it is possible to build enduring companies that evolve, if they provide a persistent service like energy and food. The variable isn't the vessel. It is the permanence of what is served.

So the turtles end here, for now: no first cause, no bedrock, no final turtle. Instead — a vastness to hold, people to hold it with, a source to draw from, a machine to do the walking, and a mark to leave in the people and the ground that outlast you.

Onward.
