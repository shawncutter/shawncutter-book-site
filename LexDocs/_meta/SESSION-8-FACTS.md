This memo did something important — it made the Chuck-and-the-household pages refuse to flinch. The previous photo-driven description of Chuck described a man. This memo described **a household.** The two versions of Chuck (the brutal one Shawna never saw, the softening grandfather holding Gia in 2010) are the same man at different chapters, and the book has to hold both. The protector scene is the structural counterweight to the holding-Gia photo. Together they bracket who Chuck was.

Shawna's view of the family — "they were a good family with a real family life" — is one of the most painful lines in the book. The half-sister wanted in to what was actually a hell. The inside knew. She didn't.
