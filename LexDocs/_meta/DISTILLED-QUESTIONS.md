# DISTILLED QUESTIONS (v1 — 2026-05-13)

Ten questions. Each is one scene or one line — not a topic. Pick any and walk me into the room.

This file supersedes the dumps in `running-questions.md` and the long open-question lists at the bottom of each `*-QUESTIONS.md` file. Those are still useful as inventories. This is the ranked working set.

Ordered by **what would unlock the most narrative right now** if you sat down and recorded an answer.

---

## SCENES — walk me into the room

### 1. The Carina fight.
During the fight that broke the machine, she shared something that really upset her. **Not what the fight was about — her actual words.** What did she SAY? Where were you in the house? What were her hands doing? What did you say back, if anything?
> Unlocks: the central Foundry-arc scene. Currently a placeholder.

### 2. The rock.
You were in 4th grade. CJ was in 6th. Walking up the driveway, you ran down the steep cliff and **saw something.** What did you see? How did the rock end up in his hand? Where did it hit you? Walk me from "I saw something down" to the 30-32 stitches and what your mom said when she saw your face.
> Unlocks: the strongest pre-accident sibling-violence scene. The memo cut off mid-stride.

### 3. The ATV ride.
The morning argument with Mom is captured. The defiance line ("I could get hurt riding a bicycle") is captured. **Now the ride itself.** What were you doing in the pasture field that day? The ramp, the fence, the moment the wheels left the ground, the moment you knew you weren't going to clear it. Who found you. The drive to the hospital. Coming clean to your parents.
> Unlocks: the ATV-crash piece. Becomes the bookend to the October 1990 accident.

### 4. The first time you held Gia.
Not the labor — we have that. **The moment she was in your arms and you noticed the wall was up.** Where were you in the room? What was Carina doing? What did you say out loud, if anything? What was the silence in your chest like?
> Unlocks: the emotional-wall scene the Foundry arc needs. Currently described from outside; needs the inside view.

### 5. One Mario Harik moment at Tallan.
Pick one scene. The day he hired you. A meeting where he tore something apart. A beer after work. A project crisis. The day you knew he was going somewhere. **One scene, one room.** You said "I definitely have stories" — give me the one you'd tell first.
> Unlocks: the Mario thread + the bridge between trucker-stories-with-Zeb and the man who became XPO's CEO.

### 6. The eulogy.

A line that made the room go quiet? If a written copy exists anywhere — old hard drive, email draft, printed program, mom's box of papers — try to recover it.

### 7. The first time you sounded like Chuck.

### 8. What do you know now about YOURSELF that you didn't know five years ago?
**Not business. Not philosophy. You.** Something that surprised you when you saw it. Something therapy named. Something Carina or the girls reflected back. One specific thing, said in one sentence if you can.
> Unlocks: the Foundry-arc reflection beat that closes the book.

### 9. Jobs, Gates, Musk — and the Cutter.
One sentence on what each one taught you. One sentence on what each one couldn't see. One sentence on what the Cutter version is — the org / philosophy / arc — that none of them built.
> Unlocks: the closing chapter's thesis. You've studied all three deeply; the book deserves your synthesis, not a list.

## THREADS NEEDING A FINAL BEAT

### 10. "Plug myself into the internet."
You've said it as a vision. **Now the feeling, not the tech.** What does it mean to you that a version of you could keep existing beyond your body? Is it grief about Chuck dying at 54 wearing a different face? Is it a way to be a better father to your daughters forever? Have you ever told Carina this? Have you ever told Gia?

## HOW TO USE THIS

- You don't need to answer in order.
- 3-5 minutes per question is plenty. Voice memo, free-form, don't edit.
- Pick the one that's most alive for you today. The book doesn't need them in sequence — it needs them in voice.
- If a question doesn't fit, say so — that's also useful (the wrong question often reveals the right one underneath it).
