# APPENDIX A — Gap-Finding Questions (added 2026-05-09)

*These are STRUCTURAL gap questions surfaced from co-reading "Counting Shawns" and other pieces. Run these on any draft that's near-ready. They catch the things a sentence-level edit misses.*

## A1. Scene-as-Load-Bearing
- Where in this piece is the SINGLE SCENE that has to carry the most weight (the turn, the realization, the moment everything pivots)?
- Is that scene written AS A SCENE — sensory, in-time, dialogue, body — or as summary?
- What did your hands feel like at the turn moment?
- What was the temperature, light, sound at the inflection?
- Could this scene be MOVED UP and become the cold open?

## A2. List-of-Influences Pile-Up
- If you mention 3+ named influences (philosophers, mentors, books, bands, products) in one paragraph, do they each do UNIQUE work? Or are some of them decoration?
- Which 1-2 could carry the load of all of them?
- Are the rest costume?

## A3. Internal Consistency Drift
- Does any concept (named character, framework, list, number) appear in two places with slightly different attributes? (Example: Counting Shawns top says "#5 grunge and pissed"; bottom says "#5 wanted to be seen" — drift.)
- If you re-state a frame, is the second appearance a deliberate EVOLUTION or accidental drift?
- Pick one frame and hold it both places — or make the second mention an explicit reinterpretation.

## A4. Named People Specificity
- "The people closest to me" — who SPECIFICALLY? Even one named person makes the abstraction bite.
- Is there a real person in this piece who deserves to appear by name?
- If anonymized — is it for privacy or laziness?
- Could ONE specific named person replace the whole abstraction?

## A5. Whitespace / Beat Structure
- Is any paragraph longer than 6-8 lines? If yes, are there competing beats inside?
- Where does the reader's eye need to rest? Add white space.
- What's the visual rhythm? (Mix of short, medium, occasional long.)
- Read the piece WITHOUT the prose — just the paragraph shapes. Does it look readable?

## A6. Economic Re-Use / Doubling
- Does the piece state its takeaway twice (once in the body, once in the closing summary)? Cut the weaker one.
- Is the rule/principle ALSO in the closing? Choose where it lives best.
- Does any frame appear three times? Once is setup, twice is reinforcement, three is preachy.

## A7. Hook-as-Cold-Open
- Does the piece OPEN in scene? Or with abstraction/setup/throat-clearing?
- Could the FINAL scene be moved up and become the cold open?
- If the first sentence were removed, would the second sentence work as the open?
- Could the title be a line FROM the piece rather than a label ABOUT it?

## A8. The "I Want to Know More" Test
- After reading: what's the ONE question I'm carrying that the piece didn't answer?
- Should that question have been answered? Or is it deliberately left open?
- If deliberately open — is the openness EARNED?

## A9. Voice Drift Check
- Does the voice flatten anywhere? (Sentences get longer, vocabulary gets safer, generic memoir-speak creeps in.)
- Where does the piece sound like ANY founder, vs. only Shawn?
- Mark the spots where you're hiding behind a word.

## A10. Dual-Tier Extraction (free + paid)
- Is there a SCENE that almost made it but you cut for the Medium piece? → Paid tier candidate.
- Is there a deeper context, a fact pack, a writer's commentary that would interest the 10% but bore the 90%? → Paid tier.
- Is there a voice-memo annotation that shows your THINKING about the piece? → Paid tier.
- The free tier is the polished story. The paid tier is the kitchen with the lights on.

# APPENDIX C — Voice Memo → Dual-Tier Workflow

*For any piece that originated from a voice memo (which is most of them now):*

After the polished free-tier piece is published:
- Save the original transcript as `[piece-slug]-voicememo.md` in the same arc folder
- In the paid-tier companion piece, include:
  1. The transcript with margin notes (your thinking shown)
  2. The scene that almost made it (with explanation of why cut)
  3. The questions you asked yourself while writing
  4. What you'd write differently if you wrote it again
  5. "Release notes vNext" — what's in draft for the next iteration

This is the writer-keeps-the-tradeoffs-visible move. Free tier is the polished decision. Paid tier is the conversation about the decision.
