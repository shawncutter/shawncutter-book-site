## 👤 PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION (photo-confirmed)

Built thick — stocky, broad shoulders, barrel chest, thick forearms, hairy chest. ~5'10"–5'11". Dark brown hair: shoulder-length and shaggy as a teen/groom (1974 graduation, late-70s wedding photo), then cut short and lived under a ball cap for the rest of his life — he was ashamed of going bald and hid it under the hat. The signature feature is the thick dark mustache, going gray by his late forties. Greenish-hazel eyes that stuck out — the eyes Shawn inherited. Strong jaw, prominent brow, thick neck. Weathered tan from oilfield and Florida sun. Big thick hands.

**Uniform:** a Cutter Oil ball cap (pink, black, or yellow versions with the pump-jack logo — or a generic red cap), white cotton t-shirt or sleeveless cutoff with Marlboros in the breast pocket, gold chain at the collar, sunglasses hung at the chest, jeans, work boots. Carhartt in winter.

## 🎭 WHO HE WAS

### The Public Chuck
- **Dominant presence.** The loudest person in any room. The one who got the stories told about him.
- **Oilfield operator.** Ran Cutter Oil — trucks, rigs, field work. Shawn grew up riding in his truck, absorbing the language of day rates, call-outs, standby pay, AFEs.
- **Hard worker.** Expected the same from his boys. Farm chores, animal responsibilities, early mornings.
- **Intense.** Not gentle. Not soft. But effective.

### The Private Chuck
- **Motorcycle accident** left him with knee injuries that later contributed to his death (exacerbated blockage → stents → blood thinners).
- **Infidelity — and where.** Shawna's mother worked at **Country Acres Restaurant** — the restaurant Beth's parents started, where Beth and her sisters had grown up working. Chuck's affair wasn't just outside the marriage. It was inside Beth's family business. That's the layer that makes it a particular kind of cruelty, not just a betrayal.
- **The day Beth caught him.** Beth loaded the kids in the car and drove to find his truck parked at the other woman's house. He saw her drive by. **When he got home, he was pissed at HER for catching him.** Started yelling around the house. The kids yelled at him to be quiet. He turned toward the kids. Beth stepped in front and told him: *"You won't touch these kids."* He didn't cross that line. But the fact that the line had to be drawn — that the kids were the ones backed up against the wall while the wife stood between them and the husband on the day she'd just confirmed the affair — is who Chuck was in the worst version.
- **The household inside, vs. the household outside.** Shawna, looking in from the outside, thought the Cutters were a good family with a real family life. The inside reality, per Shawn (2026-05-13): "Pretty fucking terrible. A lot of work. A lot of yelling. Not a nice environment to be a part of a lot of the times." Chuck ran the household at that pitch. The "softening grandfather holding Gia" version of Chuck (2010) and this version are the same man at different chapters. The book has to hold both without flinching from either.
- **Getting softer as he got older.** Session 5 confirmed: "Chuck was getting softer as he was getting older. I was really looking forward to him being a grandpa."
- **Met Gia in May 2010** — had about 6 months with her before dying Nov 12.
- **Would have been a great grandpa** — a version of Chuck the family never got to fully see.

### Chuck as a Father
- **Took Shawn everywhere.** Rides in the truck, oilfield visits, wing spots (BW3, sports bars on I-71 in Cleveland).
- **Zero-G flight with Shawn** — went up in the Cessna, Shawn did full pre-flight, Chuck was "super quiet." Did zero-G's, spooked him. Went for wings after at a BBQ place (name unknown).
- **Expected toughness.** Farm work, independence, responsibility. Tractor pulls were "dad's thing." Boys had to sit through them.
- **Physical discipline.** Beth sometimes had to protect the boys from Chuck's intensity.
- **The wrestling tournament** — one of the few overtly proud moments (Shawn returning to wrestling ~3-4 months after the accident).

## 🔗 KEY RELATIONSHIPS

### Chuck ↔ Shawn
- Core relationship of the entire book. Every arc references Chuck.
- Shawn absorbed Chuck's operating system — the work ethic, the intensity, the oilfield knowledge that became Fielding's secret weapon.
- Chuck took Shawn up in a plane. The flight with Chuck is one of the emotional peaks of Forging.
- "I didn't start sounding like Chuck until the kids were maybe 4 or 5."
- Shawn was looking forward to Chuck being a grandpa. Never got that.

### Chuck ↔ Beth
- Beth endured Chuck's intensity, his infidelity (Shawna), and his death.
- She was sometimes the protector — shielding the boys from Chuck's worst moments.
- "Eternal love in her heart" despite everything.

### Chuck ↔ CJ
- Different dynamic than with Shawn. (Details sparse — CJ recording questions may fill this in.)

- The version of Chuck that was softening — the grandpa they barely got to know.
