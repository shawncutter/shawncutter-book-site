# The Wooster Steer

My dad took a Holstein steer — a full-grown black-and-white steer, the kind you halter and lead — loaded it up, hauled it into the city of Wooster, Ohio, and tied it in a friend's front yard.

Not a pasture. Not a barn lot. A *front yard.* In town. Grass, sidewalk, neighbors, the whole deal. A thousand pounds of dairy cattle staked out on somebody's lawn like a lawn ornament that could kick a hole through your garage.

I want to sit with that for a second, because it's the most efficient possible summary of who Chuck Cutter was.

Most people, when they want to prank a friend, they think small. Toilet paper. A fake for-sale sign. Something you can undo in ten minutes. My dad's brain didn't work in that register. My dad's brain went: *I have access to livestock, a trailer, and no fear of consequences whatsoever — what's the biggest, most absurd, most logistically insane thing I could deposit on this man's property?* And then he actually did it. That's the part that separates him from every other guy who ever had the same dumb idea over a beer. He *executed.* He got the steer, he got the trailer, he drove it into town, he led it up onto the lawn, and he tied it off and left.

Think about the steps. You have to catch and load a steer, which is not nothing. You have to drive it through a residential area with a cow in the trailer. You have to unload it without it dragging you down the street. You have to secure it. And at every single step, a normal person hits a moment where they go, "this is insane, let's not." He never hit that moment. That moment didn't exist in him. That's the whole man — measured chances taken with total commitment, and a complete willingness to step over the line that most people won't even walk up to.

This is the operating system I grew up inside. Crass, larger than life, able to do the thing other people only joke about. And here's what I've come to understand as an adult building companies: that exact wiring — the part that puts a steer on a lawn — is the same part that lets you cold-call an oilfield and make it work, that lets you bet the farm on something nobody else can see. The line between "menace" and "founder" is thinner than anybody admits. My dad lived right on it.

I don't know if the friend laughed. I hope he did. Knowing the crowd my dad ran with, he probably tied one up in *Chuck's* yard three weeks later and called it even.

But the image is permanent. A Holstein steer, standing in the middle of a manicured front yard in Wooster, Ohio, chewing, unbothered, waiting for someone to figure out what my father had done. That's the picture. That's the guy.
